What you showed us, and what drives the price

We reviewed both reference videos you shared. Together they describe one product with two quality bars, and we have priced against both.

Reference 1 · the product
  • Guest scans a table QR and opens a branded web menu
  • Categories, search, veg / non-veg filters, translation
  • "View in AR" places the dish on the real table
  • Floating ingredient tags on the dish (e.g. Tomato · Vitamin C)
Reference 2 · the quality bar
  • AR dish matches the real plate when it arrives
  • True-to-size portion, real textures, correct plateware
  • Used live in a working restaurant, on guests' own phones
  • This needs on-site 3D scanning of each dish, not stock models

The main cost driver is the number of dishes in 3D. Each dish is photographed from 100–200 angles on-site, rebuilt as a 3D model, cleaned up, and compressed to under 4 MB so it loads fast on mobile data. The menu software itself is our reusable platform, which keeps your setup fee low.

Why it pays back

Guests order with more confidence when they can see the portion and plating, and AR makes your signature and higher-priced dishes easier to sell. Our analytics show you which dishes guests look at and which they skip, so you can adjust the menu with data.

Illustration, not a promise: the Signature plan costs about ₹200 a day. If AR helps you sell two extra desserts or one upgraded main a day, the plan covers itself. We agree target numbers with you at kickoff and report against them monthly.

Notes

  • On-site scanning within city limits is included. Outstation visits are billed at actual travel cost.
  • AR opens in the phone's browser. iPhones use Apple's built-in AR viewer; most Android phones from the last five years are supported. Older phones still see the full menu with photos and a 3D view that rotates on screen.
  • Dishes that change appearance a lot (soups, drinks with ice, live-plated items) may be shown as 3D-on-screen instead of AR. We'll flag these during discovery.
  • You own your menu content, photos and the 3D models of your dishes. The software platform remains Divex Labs IP unless source-code ownership is purchased.
